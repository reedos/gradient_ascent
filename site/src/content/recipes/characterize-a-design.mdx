Five hand-built Orbeck SRB-5030 revision C boards came back from the board house. The design
review is Thursday, and nobody knows yet what the regulator does at 9 V in, 3 A out and 70 degC,
which is the corner where low line, full load and a hot ambient all push the output the same way.
This is engineering test, not production: five boards, not two hundred, a script that runs a few
times over three days rather than a fixed sequence run on every unit, and a report at the end
rather than a per-unit pass or fail. The artifacts on hand are
`evals/bench/data/characterization-2026-09.csv` (four input voltages, three load currents, three
ambients, five readings a point, no verdict column at all) and
`evals/bench/corpus/characterization-notebook.md`, the engineer's own working notes.

## Level 0, and why not higher

A sweep over line, load and temperature is a nested loop. The margin to a datasheet limit at a
corner is a subtraction. An uncertainty budget for one reading is a root sum of squares over four
named numbers, and guardbanding a limit by it is another subtraction. Finding the corner where a
board's margin is thinnest, of the 36 this sweep visits for each of the five boards, is a
minimum. None of it needs a model, for the same reason
<Link href="/recipes/limits-without-a-model/">limits without a model</Link> needs none on the
production log: a margin nobody can reproduce is worse than no margin, and an uncertainty a model
assembled looks exactly like one a budget produced, so neither may ever come from one.

Low volume changes what the arithmetic is for, not whether it is arithmetic. There is no golden
run to check five boards against and no population to run statistics over: no Cpk and no control
chart, because both need more units than a characterization sweep will ever have. A margin and an
uncertainty budget do that work here instead, one board at a time.

A model earns a place once the job stops being arithmetic over numbers this sweep already
produced. Turning the notebook and these results into a report a reviewer can read is
<Link href="/recipes/measurement-writeup/">measurement writeup</Link>, one call, with every
figure in the draft checked against what code computed. Getting the meter's accuracy table out of
its programming manual in the first place is
<Link href="/recipes/accuracy-specs-from-the-manual/">accuracy specs from the manual</Link>,
which is where the rows the budget below prices a reading against come from. This page has no
model call anywhere in it.

## The sweep, stepped

Nothing here talks to an instrument. The MDN-6100 already wrote every reading to
`characterization-2026-09.csv`, and this recipe starts after that, reading the file the way
`evals.bench.CHARACTERIZATION_CSV` names it. A row is a reading and not a verdict: no `result`
column and no limit column, because the limit a margin is taken against lives in the datasheet.
That limit is the 4.900 to 5.100 V window `srb5030-datasheet.md` section 4 holds over the full
line, load and temperature range, not the tighter 4.9500 to 5.0500 V window production checks at
one fixed condition.

Grouping every corner by board and keeping the smallest margin finds the same corner for all five
boards, without being told where to look:

| Serial | VOUT at 9.0 V, 3.000 A, 70 degC | Margin to 4.900 V |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SRB5030-2609-0001 | 4.97391 V | 73.9 mV |
| SRB5030-2609-0002 | 4.95822 V | 58.2 mV |
| SRB5030-2609-0003 | 4.92038 V | 20.4 mV |
| SRB5030-2609-0004 | 4.97849 V | 78.5 mV |
| SRB5030-2609-0005 | 4.96470 V | 64.7 mV |

Board SRB5030-2609-0003 is not a failing board. It passes every corner in the file, its output at
24 V in and 1 A is 4.9585 V against the 4.950 to 5.050 V window that condition is checked against,
its load regulation is 0.657 percent against an 0.800 percent limit, and its line regulation is
0.236 percent against 0.300. No single parameter of it is out of specification. What it does not
have is much left over where all three push the same way. That is the finding, and a sample of one
typical board at 25 degC would have said nothing about it.

For board 3's five readings at its worst corner, 9.0 V in, 3.000 A out, 70 degC ambient, on the
10 V range the sweep script selected:

| Contribution | Half-width or spread | Standard uncertainty |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Meter accuracy, 1 year, 10 V range, 23 degC | 222.2 uV | 128.3 uV |
| Resolution, 10 uV per count | 5.0 uV | 2.9 uV |
| Repeatability, 5 readings | s = 80.7 uV | 36.1 uV |
| Leads and connections | 200 uV | 115.5 uV |
| Combined | | 176.4 uV |
| Expanded, k = 2 | | 352.7 uV |

The first column is what the specification or the record allows; the second is that reduced to a
standard uncertainty. An accuracy specification is a limit and says nothing about where inside it
this meter sits, so it is treated as a rectangular distribution and divided by the square root of
3, and so is the display's own quantization, whose half-width is half a count. Repeatability is
the one line measured on the day rather than looked up: the sample standard deviation of the five
readings over the square root of five. The four combine by root sum of squares, and k = 2 covers
roughly 95 percent of where the true value could be once they are combined, which is a coverage
statement and not a guarantee that the value is inside.

The 23 degC in the first line is the meter's own ambient, not the chamber's: the boards sat at
70 degC and the meter sat next to the operator (`characterization-notebook.md` section 6 names
both). And the 352.7 uV belongs to those five readings and to no others, which is worth saying
because two nearby figures are quoted elsewhere for other readings: the meter's manual works a
budget for ten readings of a different rail and gets 349.5 uV, and the notebook works one for a
generic reading of this session and gets 351.6 uV. All three are honest and none of them is
interchangeable.

Against 20.4 mV of margin, 352.7 uV is nothing: the margin is about 58 times the measurement,
which is what makes this a design finding rather than a coincidence of where the meter happened to
land that day. `guarded_verdict` calls it a pass, correctly, and the page would be wrong to call
it more than that: a thin margin is a design question for Thursday's review, not a test failure.

Guardbanding is not only for the corner that looks tight. Running the same check over every
board's line regulation at every ambient, rather than only the case the notebook flags, finds a
second board with nothing wrong at its nominal corner:

| Serial | Ambient | Line regulation | Uncertainty | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| SRB5030-2609-0005 | 0 degC | 0.267% | +/-0.0075 points | pass |
| SRB5030-2609-0005 | 25 degC | 0.299% | +/-0.0075 points | cannot say |
| SRB5030-2609-0005 | 70 degC | 0.334% | +/-0.0076 points | fail |

The 25 degC row is the one this page exists to teach. A bare comparison against the 0.300 percent
limit says 0.299 is a pass, and that comparison is not wrong about the arithmetic; it is wrong
about what the measurement can support. Line regulation is a difference of two readings through
the same leads, so the lead-and-connection line cancels and the uncertainty is smaller than a
single reading's: 0.0075 percentage points, expanded at k = 2, against a margin of 0.0006 points.
Guardbanded, the acceptance limit is 0.2925 percent and 0.299 is over it, so the honest statement
is that this measurement does not say whether that board meets its line regulation limit at
25 degC. It is not a pass and it is not a fail. The 70 degC row is not ambiguous at all, 0.334
against 0.300 by far more than the measurement is worth, so the design question board 5 raises is
real whatever the 25 degC row can or cannot say.

The other twelve checks are passes, and not all the same kind of pass, which is why the scan
reports the distance rather than only the word. Boards 1, 2 and 4 clear the limit by about 25
times the uncertainty, board 3 by nine, and board 5's own 0 degC row by four. Two of the twelve,
both at 25 degC, carry four times the usual uncertainty for a reason that has nothing to do with
the boards, which is the next finding.

That finding is not about any board. Grouping the output voltage's point-to-point spread by the
`meter_range_v` column every row already carries, rather than by board or by corner, finds sixty
readings that scatter about seven times more than the rest:

| Meter range | Points | Mean spread | Boards |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 10.0 V | 168 | 58.6 uV | 5 |
| 100.0 V | 12 | 413.9 uV | 2 |

Nothing about those sixty readings is an error: the error queue is empty and the numbers are
right. They are just worth less, because the meter's accuracy specification is per range and the
100 V range's term does not scale down for a 5 V reading. Pricing the same five readings both
ways puts a number on that: 2.4 times the expanded uncertainty. The window crosses a board
boundary, so two boards are touched and each only partly, which is what rules the boards
themselves out: a board with a repeatability problem would be noisy at every corner, and these two
are noisy only where the meter sat on the wrong range.

That window is also why two of the regulation checks carry 0.031 points instead of 0.0075. One end
of each of those figures was read on the 100 V range and the other on the 10 V range, so
`scan_line_regulation` prices the pair on the coarser of the two: a difference is no better than
the weaker half of it. Pricing them on the range the sweep script meant to use would have quoted a
100 V reading against a 10 V row, which is the same mistake one size down.

## How to evaluate it

<HowToEval>

There is no free-text answer here for a person to grade, so there is no confusion matrix: a
margin, a budget and a guardbanded verdict are closed-form arithmetic, and either they reproduce
the numbers `docs/THE-BENCH.md` and the notebook state or there is a bug.
`tests/test_example_bench_characterize_a_design.py` checks every number this page states two
ways, once by calling the module and once by recomputing it independently from the raw CSV with
nothing but `csv`, `statistics` and `math`. At five boards there is no golden run to score against
and no population to draw a sample size from, so what a person checks by hand is what those tests
check: that the corner the page calls the worst one really is the minimum over every corner in
the file, that a verdict marked cannot say is not a pass with a softer name, and that every budget
was priced on the range and the calibration interval its readings actually carry.
`scripts/eval_run.py`'s 60-question set grades a drafted answer against a document set; nothing
here drafts an answer, so `scripts/eval_run.py bench_characterize_a_design` says that rather than
pretending to score it.

</HowToEval>

## How to adapt it

Every number above, the 4.900 to 5.100 V window, the four input voltages and three load currents,
the five serials, is this bench's own. A real sweep has its own window and its own corners, and
the same code takes them from the datasheet and from the file rather than from a list typed into
a page: `window_margin` takes the limits as arguments, and `worst_corner_per_board` and
`scan_line_regulation` derive the conditions to check from the file's own columns.

The lead-and-connection contribution is a number from `calibration-procedure.md`, carried over
from a fixture path this session's bench harness is not.
`characterization-notebook.md` section 7 says so directly: that line is assumed and not measured,
and everything downstream of it moves if it is wrong. A real budget states where each of its four
lines came from with the same honesty, including the one that is a guess.

This shape is not specific to electronics. Any job with a small number of prototypes swept over
several conditions, where the answer is a margin and not a verdict and there are too few units for
a population statistic to mean anything, is the same reasoning: a material's strength across a
batch of coupons at several temperatures, a chemical process's yield across a handful of pilot
runs at different feed rates, a mechanical tolerance stack checked across a small first-article
build. None of those needs a model either.
