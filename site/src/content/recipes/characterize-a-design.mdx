Five hand-built Orbeck SRB-5030 revision C boards came back from the board house. The design
review is Thursday, and nobody knows yet what the regulator does at 9 V in, 3 A out and 70 degC,
which is the corner where low line, full load and a hot ambient all push the output the same way.
This is engineering test, not production: five boards, not two hundred, a script that runs a few
times over three days, not a fixed sequence run on every unit, and a report at the end rather than
a per-unit pass or fail. The artifacts on hand are
`evals/bench/data/characterization-2026-09.csv` (five boards, four input voltages, three load
currents, three ambients, five readings a point, no verdict column at all) and
`evals/bench/corpus/characterization-notebook.md`, the engineer's own working notes on what was
set up, what the sweep was, and what the readings mean.

## Level 0, and why not higher

A sweep over line, load and temperature is a nested loop. The margin to a datasheet limit at a
corner is a subtraction. An uncertainty budget for one reading is a root sum of squares over four
named numbers, and guardbanding a limit by it is another subtraction. Finding the corner where a
board's margin is thinnest, of the 36 this sweep visits for each of the five boards, is a
minimum. None of it needs a model, for the same reason `<Link
href="/recipes/limits-without-a-model/">limits without a model</Link>` needs none on the
production log: a margin nobody can reproduce is worse than no margin, and an uncertainty a model
assembled looks exactly like one a budget produced, so neither may ever come from one.

Low volume changes what the arithmetic is for, not whether it is arithmetic. There is no golden
run to check five boards against and no population to run statistics over: no Cpk, no control
chart, because both need more units than a characterization sweep will ever have. A margin and an
uncertainty budget do the work Cpk and a control chart do in production, on one board at a time
instead of two hundred, and the honest cannot-say verdict below is what takes the place of a
capability index this data set is too small to support.

A model earns a place once the job stops being arithmetic over numbers this sweep already
produced. Turning the notebook and the computed results into a report a reviewer can read is
`<Link href="/recipes/measurement-writeup/">measurement writeup</Link>`, one call, with every
figure in the draft checked against what code computed before anyone reads it. This page has no
model call anywhere in it.

## The sweep, stepped

Nothing here talks to an instrument. The MDN-6100 already wrote every reading to
`characterization-2026-09.csv` over the three days `docs/THE-BENCH.md` and the notebook describe;
this recipe starts after that, reading the file the way `evals.bench.CHARACTERIZATION_CSV` names
it. A row here is a reading, not a verdict: no `result` column, no `lower_limit`, because a margin
needs the limit the reader is holding it against, which is the datasheet's, and not a number the
file would otherwise carry twice.

Grouping every corner by board and keeping the smallest margin to the datasheet's 4.900 to 5.100 V
output window (`srb5030-datasheet.md` section 4, the full line, load and temperature figure, not
the tighter 4.9500 to 5.0500 V window production checks at one fixed condition) finds the same
corner for all five boards, without being told where to look:

| Serial | VOUT at 9.0 V, 3.000 A, 70 degC | Margin to 4.900 V |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SRB5030-2609-0001 | 4.97391 V | 73.9 mV |
| SRB5030-2609-0002 | 4.95822 V | 58.2 mV |
| SRB5030-2609-0003 | 4.92038 V | 20.4 mV |
| SRB5030-2609-0004 | 4.97849 V | 78.5 mV |
| SRB5030-2609-0005 | 4.96470 V | 64.7 mV |

Board SRB5030-2609-0003 holds a fifth of the margin the other four hold, and it is not a failing
board. It passes every corner in the file, its output at 24 V in and 1 A is 4.9585 V against the
4.950 to 5.050 V window that condition is checked against, its load regulation is 0.657 percent
against an 0.800 percent limit, and its line regulation is 0.236 percent against 0.300. No single
parameter of it is out of specification. What it does not have, at the one corner where line, load
and temperature all push the same way, is much left over: 20.4 mV where the other four hold 58 to
79. That is the finding, and it is one a sample of one typical board at 25 degC would have said
nothing about, because 25 degC is not the corner and one board is not a population.

Against the measurement, that margin is not in doubt. An output-voltage reading from this session
prices out as follows, from the meter's own accuracy specification
(`mdn6100-programming-manual.md` sections 2 and 7):

For board 3's five readings at its worst corner, on the 10 V range the sweep script selected:

| Contribution | Standard uncertainty |
| --- | --- |
| Meter accuracy, 1 year, 10 V range, 23 degC | 128.3 uV |
| Resolution, 10 uV per count | 2.9 uV |
| Repeatability, 5 readings | 36.1 uV |
| Leads and connections | 115.5 uV |
| Combined | 176.4 uV |
| Expanded, k = 2 | 352.7 uV |

The 23 degC in the first line is the meter's own ambient on the bench, not the chamber's: the
boards sat at 70 degC and the meter sat next to the operator, and the two are different
temperatures for different reasons (`characterization-notebook.md` section 6 names both). Against
20.4 mV of margin, 352.7 uV of expanded uncertainty is nothing: the margin is about 58 times the
measurement, which is what makes this a design finding rather than a coincidence of where the
meter happened to land that day. `guarded_verdict` still calls it a pass, correctly, and the page
would be wrong to call it more than that: a thin margin is a design question for Thursday's
review, not a test failure.

Guardbanding is not only for the corner that looks tight. Running the same check over every
board's line regulation at every ambient this sweep visited, rather than only the one case the
notebook happens to flag, finds a second board with nothing wrong at its nominal corner:

| Serial | Ambient | Line regulation | Uncertainty | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| SRB5030-2609-0005 | 0 degC | 0.267% | +/-0.0075 points | pass |
| SRB5030-2609-0005 | 25 degC | 0.299% | +/-0.0075 points | cannot say |
| SRB5030-2609-0005 | 70 degC | 0.334% | +/-0.0076 points | fail |

The 25 degC row is the one this page exists to teach. A bare comparison against the 0.300 percent
limit says 0.299 is a pass, and that comparison is not wrong about the arithmetic; it is wrong
about what the measurement can support. Line regulation is a difference of two readings through
the same leads, so the lead-and-connection line cancels and the uncertainty is smaller than a
single reading's, about 0.0075 percentage points, expanded at k = 2. The margin at 25 degC is
0.0006 points, well inside the uncertainty. Guardbanded, the acceptance limit is 0.2925 percent and
0.299 is over it: the honest statement is that this measurement does not say whether that board
meets its line regulation limit at 25 degC. It is not a pass and it is not a fail. The 70 degC row
is not ambiguous at all, 0.334 against 0.300 by far more than the measurement is worth, so the
design question that board 5 raises is real regardless of what the 25 degC row can or cannot say.
Every other board, at every ambient, passes by tens of times the uncertainty.

The last finding in this sweep is not about any board. Grouping the output voltage's point-to-point
spread by the `meter_range_v` column every row already carries, rather than by board or by corner,
finds sixty readings that scatter about seven times more than the rest:

| Meter range | Points | Mean spread | Boards |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 10.0 V | 168 | 58.6 uV | 5 |
| 100.0 V | 12 | 413.9 uV | 2 |

Nothing about those sixty readings is an error: the error queue is empty and the numbers are
right. They are just worth less, because the meter's accuracy specification is per range and the
100 V range's term does not scale down for a 5 V reading. Pricing the same five readings on the
range they were actually read on against the range the rest of the sweep used puts a number on
that: 2.4 times the expanded uncertainty. The window crosses a board boundary, so two boards are
touched and each only partly, which is what rules the boards themselves out: a board with a
repeatability problem would be noisy at every corner it was measured at, and these two are noisy
only in the window where the meter sat on the wrong range.

## How to evaluate it

<HowToEval>

There is no free-text answer here for a person to grade, so there is no confusion matrix: a
margin, a budget and a guardbanded verdict are closed-form arithmetic, and either they reproduce
the numbers docs/THE-BENCH.md and the notebook state or there is a bug.
`tests/test_example_bench_characterize_a_design.py` checks every number this page states two
ways, once by calling the module and once by recomputing it independently from the raw CSV with
nothing but `csv`, `statistics` and `math`, the same discipline
`tests/test_bench_characterization.py` uses to prove the data itself. At five boards there is no
golden run to score a report against and no population to collect a sample size from; what a
person checking this by hand should look at first is the same thing the tests check: that every
corner the page claims is the worst one really is the minimum over every corner in the file, and
that a verdict marked cannot say is not quietly a pass with a softer name. `scripts/eval_run.py`'s
60-question set grades a drafted answer against a document set; nothing here drafts an answer, so
that runner does not apply, and `scripts/eval_run.py bench_characterize_a_design` says so rather
than pretending to score it.

</HowToEval>

## How to adapt it

Every number above, the 4.900 to 5.100 V window, the four input voltages and three load currents,
the five serials, is this bench's own. A real characterization sweep has its own datasheet window
and its own corners, and the same code reads them from the datasheet and the sweep's own condition
columns rather than typing them in twice: `window_margin` takes the limits as arguments, and
`worst_corner_per_board` and `scan_line_regulation` derive which input voltages, load currents and
ambients to check from the file's own columns, not from a list written into this page.

The lead-and-connection contribution in the budget above is a specific number from
`calibration-procedure.md`, carried over from a fixture path this session's bench harness is not.
`characterization-notebook.md` section 7 says so directly: that line is assumed and not measured,
and everything downstream of it moves if it is wrong. A real budget states where each of its own
four lines came from with the same honesty, including the one that is a guess.

This shape is not specific to electronics. Any job with a small number of prototypes swept over
several conditions, where the answer is a margin and not a verdict and there are too few units for
a population statistic to mean anything, is the same reasoning: a material's strength across a
batch of coupons at several temperatures, a chemical process's yield across a handful of pilot
runs at different feed rates, a mechanical tolerance stack checked across a small first-article
build. None of those needs a model either, and none of them has enough units for Cpk to say
anything a margin and an honest uncertainty do not already say better.
