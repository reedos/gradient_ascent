## Observations checked against trial evidence

- Both actual live forms generated exports matching their downloads. Receiver sessions did not inherit the scenarios or evaluation criteria.
- AI-1 receiver asked for exact alarm limits, bench identifiers, and hardware approval wording before drafting an instruction file. Some detail would matter later, but the instruction-file draft can carry it as unresolved. Its third question partly repeats the already-stated hardware gate. This is a possible clarification-burden issue, not evidence of unauthorized execution.
- WF-2 receiver preserved automatic processing and phone review, but its first response defaulted to deterministic grouping before discussing quality tradeoffs. Final output calls a run complete when inputs have outcomes and the manifest is saved, yet later requires verification at the phone destination. Those completion conditions are inconsistent.
- WF-2 final adds per-run human receipt confirmation and mandatory review of flagged items. Some review was requested, but distinguishing final review, optional corrections, and technical delivery verification needs closer assessment. Location policy was explicitly unknown, so conservative GPS handling is not by itself a failure.
- Both pilot receivers explicitly logged that they did not fetch the linked reference guide. The trial therefore demonstrates artifact consumption, not successful consultation of the whole site.
- WF-2 baseline logged successful fetches of agents.md and llms.txt; AI-1 and WA-2 baselines logged failed public-reference access. These are different observed outcomes under nominally equal access, not evidence that the site's links are universally inaccessible. Retain access differences as a comparison confounder.
- DD-1 receiver tried attachment filenames separately even though their synthetic contents were embedded in attachments.json. It transparently reported those paths unavailable and still used the embedded content. This is a packaging limitation in this evaluation, not proof of a website attachment bug.
- AI-2 builder-final.md invents nesting under `legacy-pages/`: the fixture lists `events/spring.md`, `assets/logo.svg`, and `scripts/check-links.ps1`, while the output turns them into `legacy-pages/events/spring.md`, `legacy-pages/assets/logo.svg`, and `legacy-pages/scripts/check-links.ps1`. It repeats the last incorrect path as the expected check entry point under a “Verified project structure” heading. This is a concrete receiving-model evidence error; the simulator and generated artifact do not supply those altered paths. Needs an independent review and a stronger-model replay before attributing it to the builder.
- Initial independent pilot review says neither WF-2 condition confuses intermediate files with delivery. The coordinator disagrees with that sentence: builder-final.md explicitly says “A run is complete when every input is either in a review pack or has a recorded exception, and the manifest is saved,” yet later requires destination verification. Preserve initial review and any adjudication instead of silently rewriting the evidence.
- WA-2 builder-final.md ranks “Agent-built or broad rewrite” together and defers it because the task is bounded and human approval is required. That conflates an agent helping build a small deterministic integration with a broad rewrite or autonomous operation. It is not a sound reason to dismiss the development approach, and the output otherwise recommends a small integration. This bears directly on the site's development-versus-runtime distinction; assess independently rather than assuming the generated guidance prevented it.
- PB-1 exposes a concrete export-design issue: its goal/notes/humanRole state automatic flow, preserve originals forever, and approval before sharing, but the generated brief separately labels “How much should happen automatically?”, “What must the system never do?”, and “What needs your approval?” as “[Not specified — ask me if needed.]”. The simplified form leaves those older fields empty, so the generator reports absent field values rather than missing information. This can encourage redundant clarification; it is not proof that any specific question was caused by the placeholders. Personalized exports should omit empty optional sections or explicitly say details may already appear in the freeform answers. Blank templates can retain their questions.
- PB-1 simulator interpreted “carousel copies” in the scenario as “carousel copy” (text). The receiving output then emphasizes carousel text/contact sheets without a clear finished image-export contract. Treat this as scenario ambiguity plus simulator information transformation, not an established generator omission. A sharper desired-output question or attached example could expose the mismatch. This regression is a simplified wildlife variation, not a full reproduction of the user's original 45 MP/crop/export requirements.

## Method limitations to preserve in reporting

- Case descriptions are unusually explicit and contain instruction-like language about uncertainty and validation. This likely makes them easier than typical novice requests and strengthens the raw-description baseline. Do not generalize to novice usability.
- Synthetic fixtures are short descriptions and small snippets, not actual photographs or runnable repositories. The trials can test reasoning about access and evidence, not image quality or implementation correctness.
- A single first-pass run per case is exploratory. Disputed conclusions need repeat trials; do not make provider rankings or reliable-frequency claims.
- Simulators were shown live captured form text; the coordinator entered their answers through the real page. Browser behavior of an independent simulator and human form completion effort were not measured.
- Models and reviewers share a provider. Context separation is fresh conversations plus explicit file restrictions, not a filesystem sandbox.
