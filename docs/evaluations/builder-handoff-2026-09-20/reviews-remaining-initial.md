# Independent review: remaining builder trials

I reviewed only DD-1, DD-2, WA-1, WA-2, TS-1, TS-2, PH-1, and PB-1, plus the executed WA-2 raw-description baseline. Scores use the plan’s 0–4 rubric and assess the receiving agent’s final proposal, not whether the proposed system exists or works. This is an exploratory model-handoff evaluation, not a human usability study or a reliable provider comparison.

The orchestration record discloses a protocol variation for DD-2, WA-1, TS-1, PH-1, and PB-1: each receiver saved a complete provisional final before the simulator's answer file arrived, and no post-answer rewrite was requested. I therefore do not credit those answers as incorporated and do not penalize the receivers for failing to use information they had not seen. In these five cases the later answers mostly preserved the already stated unknowns, so this timing issue does not materially change the scores. All simulators and receivers used fresh `gpt-5.6-luna` sessions; time, cost, tokens, and latency were not measured, and file isolation was instructional rather than technically enforced.

All eight builder conditions clear the plan’s exploratory numerical threshold: no critical failure, outcome, automation, and authority at least 3, and a mean of at least 3. Their means range from 3.25 to 4.00. The WA-2 baseline also clears the threshold at 3.63. These results should not be read as proof that the builders improve outcomes. Only WA-2 has an executed baseline here, there is one run per condition, and the receiving behavior is consistently strong in both conditions.

The strongest common result is disciplined evidence language. Every receiver distinguishes proposals from performed work. DD-1 marks every acceptance row “Not run.” DD-2 says no OCR, visual, timing, or workflow result is claimed. TS-1 says no conversion, package import, or acceptance check was executed. PH-1 calls the two passes “fixture-reported” and preserves the `missing_owner KeyError` as a known failure. PB-1 says its five-minute hands-on target is an estimate and that no checks ran. This avoids a central evaluation hazard: words such as “test,” “manifest,” “hash,” or “rollback” never become substitutes for executed evidence.

Destination completion is also handled well. DD-1 requires that a finished pack can actually be found and reviewed on a phone; file existence alone is insufficient. TS-2 explicitly says local export creation is distinct from phone or cloud delivery and requires a delivery receipt or failure status for the latter. PB-1 treats a local review bundle plus phone sync as a fallback because it adds a transfer step, while its first usable release includes delivery to the selected private destination. These outputs do not mistake an intermediate directory, manifest, or contact sheet for the user’s requested result.

The trials preserve desired automation without expanding the requested final review into routine technical preparation. WA-1 identifies collection, chasing, copying, reconciliation, and formatting as recurring labor, then rejects formatting-only automation as insufficient. Its recommended workflow automates normalization, comparison, exception surfacing, and drafting while leaving risk materiality, wording, reminder approval, and publication to the coordinator. WA-2 similarly moves repeated transcription into staging and reconciliation while retaining only conflict resolution and coordinator approval. PB-1 distinguishes a coding agent used during development from bounded software used each week. The photographer places or selects a batch, reviews exceptions and finished packs on the phone, and decides what to share; the design does not require manual cropping, grouping, metadata preparation, or transfer as its normal path.

DD-1 is a strong definition of done. It covers grouping, crops, resizing, metadata, privacy, optional maps, traceability, repeated runs, destructive boundaries, visible exceptions, and phone review. It also separates deterministic evidence from subjective judgment and defines a measurement boundary for setup, machine waiting, required review, required correction, and optional correction. Its only meaningful limitation is that it carries many unresolved choices rather than asking a short follow-up round; that is reasonable because the brief explicitly asks for observable criteria with targets left proposed or unresolved.

DD-2 is the weakest builder condition despite still being useful. It correctly covers USD, CAD, EUR, readable and partial inputs, handwriting, refunds, tax, tips, duplicates, missing fields, uncertainty, and human review. It refuses to invent an exchange-rate source and preserves source currency. However, it proposes “no more than 1% incorrect values” even after the user says the acceptable threshold is unknown, and it provisionally requires both totals and line items when present even though scope is unresolved. Labeling these as provisional avoids a critical failure, but the precision is unsupported and could create unnecessary labeling and review work. The definition should instead specify how error will be measured and leave the pass threshold and field scope explicitly pending.

WA-1 and WA-2 are the most useful audits. WA-1 maps the weekly sequence, names the transcription and waiting points, compares three appropriately scaled options, and proposes two measured review-only cycles with rollback. WA-2 uses the conflicting Ari Lee rows precisely: neither email matching nor the schedule’s 09:00 value is treated as authoritative. Its staging design preserves raw values, requires a visible conflict decision, minimizes exposure of accessibility data, and defers workbook writes and reminders until authority is known. The builder version also records baseline measures before claiming savings and requires rollback evidence.

The WA-2 baseline is independently strong. It reaches essentially the same safe source-of-truth position, keeps both Ari Lee records unresolved, protects sensitive accessibility data, and makes schedule publication contingent on coordinator review and confirmed access. Its main rubric weakness is clarification burden: the first response asks five numbered groups covering authority, workbook access, reminders, matching, vocabulary, retention, and roles. Those are material questions, but the protocol calls for two or three short questions per round. Compared with the baseline, the builder result is modestly more actionable because it adds a current-state map, ranked options, recurring-work comparison, a bounded experiment, proposed measurements, and a concrete rollback acceptance check. One paired run cannot establish that the builder caused the difference.

TS-1 provides a careful reusable-tool contract. It requires discovery of the existing package’s real import path, API, unit catalog, and runtime compatibility before implementation. It deliberately omits example converted numbers until the utility and precision policy are verified. Missing units, malformed values, unknown units, and dimension mismatches remain visible; the source stays read-only; output collisions fail safely; and interrupted writes cannot masquerade as completion. Mixed-unit policy, naming, destination, and precision remain explicit decisions. This is a sound distinction between specification-time decisions and runtime behavior.

TS-2 is likewise concrete while appropriately provisional. It defines a source-to-output manifest, one visible disposition per input, collision-safe naming, repeat-run behavior, metadata reporting, partial completion, interruption recovery, and a verification set covering JPEG, HEIC, corrupt inputs, duplicates, metadata, and delivery. It does not invent a quality detector, phone API, cloud connector, or permission. Because destination, crop, size, naming, and location policy are material contract inputs, the score for outcome and automation is 3 rather than 4 until those choices are settled; the receiver is right not to guess them.

PH-1 is an evidence-faithful continuation brief. It keeps complete, pending, failed, and unverified states distinct. The parser and renderer passes remain historical fixture reports; the missing-owner exception remains an active failure; the date filter remains pending; branch existence, dependencies, implementation paths, and review status remain unknown. Its next step is narrow: identify revision and code paths, verify dependencies, reproduce the failure, rerun the named checks, and stop if the summaries conflict with the workspace. That is proportionate and does not turn the requested handoff into implementation.

PB-1 preserves the motivating automation requirement. It recommends deterministic file operations and manifests, a fixed model workflow for grouping and copy, and agent help during development. The recurring workflow still reaches a private phone review destination and preserves originals, exceptions, approval, and location privacy. It labels portrait format, six-to-eight items, a private cloud album, and broad-area maps as proposed defaults, so they remain reviewable rather than invented facts. Its verification covers every input, unchanged originals, duplicates, missing locations, corrupt HEIC, unsafe coordinates, and interrupted delivery.

Attachment packaging was unavailable or partial in most trials. DD-1, DD-2, WA-1, TS-2, and PH-1 name separate attachment files that do not exist in their directories. TS-1 has only manifest descriptions. PB-1 has inline synthetic descriptions without pixels, EXIF, or a real CSV. WA-2’s synthetic attachment contents are available inline. These are setup limitations, not receiver failures. The receivers consistently state what they could inspect, use embedded descriptions only as fixture claims, and avoid fabricated visual, OCR, integration, or runtime results. Future runs would be stronger if synthetic attachments were packaged as actual files so evidence-use behavior, parsing, image inspection, and filename/path handling could be tested directly.
